# Program Management Skill Model

## Source and interpretation

This model uses **A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide), 2000 Edition** as a durable project-management taxonomy, then translates that material into product capabilities for Program OS.

The PMBOK source is not represented here as a current PMI standard. The skill names below are Program OS design terms, not claimed PMBOK terminology.

The source emphasizes project management as an integrative endeavor where action in one area affects others, where objectives require tradeoffs, where project performance must be monitored for variance, and where corrective action and lessons learned feed future control. Those ideas are central to Program OS.

## Skill map

| Program OS skill | Priority | Product interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Program integration | Critical | Reconcile scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, stakeholders, and dependencies into one decision context |
| Schedule intelligence | Critical | Detect slippage, critical-path threats, milestone variance, and time-sensitive intervention needs |
| Risk intelligence | Critical | Identify, assess, monitor, and escalate known and emerging risks |
| Scope and change intelligence | Critical | Detect undocumented scope change and analyze downstream consequences |
| Performance monitoring | Critical | Compare plan to actual evidence and detect meaningful variance |
| Status communication | Critical | Generate evidence-backed, decision-relevant reporting |
| Decision support | Critical | Present alternatives, consequences, assumptions, and required authority |
| Stakeholder management | High | Track approvals, authority, alignment, and decision dependencies |
| Quality and readiness | High | Interpret testing, defects, readiness criteria, and hard gates |
| Cost intelligence | High | Surface budget implications and cost/schedule/scope tradeoffs |
| Resource and team intelligence | High | Detect key-person constraints, shared-resource contention, and staffing consequences |
| Procurement and vendor | Medium | Model external commitments, delivery dependencies, and contractual constraints |
| Knowledge management | High | Preserve rationale, outcomes, historical evidence, and learning |
| Organizational navigation | Human-assisted | Support escalation and authority analysis without pretending to resolve politics autonomously |
| Leadership and negotiation | Human-led | Provide evidence and options; leave persuasion, conflict resolution, and accountable judgment to humans |

## Core operating verbs

Program OS translates the skill model into seven system verbs:

**INTEGRATE → MONITOR → DETECT → ASSESS → RECOMMEND → REPORT → LEARN**

Human accountability sits across the loop.

### Monday

Integrate, monitor, detect, assess, recommend.

### During the week

Humans investigate, decide, align, and remediate while the system updates evidence.

### Thursday / Friday

Integrate, assess, report.

### After decisions

Compare recommendation, human action, and eventual outcome; propose learning where warranted.

## Integrated Impact Analysis

A material change should be evaluated across multiple objectives rather than in isolation:

~~~text
new fact or proposed change
          |
          v
scope + schedule + cost + quality + risk + stakeholder impact
          |
          v
recommended response + confidence + evidence
~~~

A scope change that appears locally beneficial may increase schedule risk, cost, testing burden, or stakeholder misalignment. Program OS should make those interactions explicit.

## Human-led boundaries

Program OS should assist but not pretend to autonomously perform:

- organizational politics;
- persuasion;
- negotiation;
- conflict mediation;
- acceptance of material business risk;
- leadership and motivation;
- final consequential decisions.

The system may identify conflict, retrieve evidence, map likely interests, prepare tradeoffs and talking points, recommend escalation, and record outcomes.
